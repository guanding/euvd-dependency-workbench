## Design Context

### Users

EU product manufacturers, Assigned Representatives, PSIRT/security staff,
compliance staff, and assessors use the workbench under evidence-heavy and
occasionally time-critical conditions. They need to screen SBOM findings,
prepare CRA Article 14 reporting material, verify it against the live ENISA
SRP, and retain an auditable human decision and receipt trail.

### Brand Personality

Precise, calm, and trustworthy. The interface should help users act with
confidence without implying legal advice, conformity, official acceptance, or
automation authority that the tool does not have.

### Aesthetic Direction

Preserve the existing light, professional workbench language: navy structure,
restrained teal actions, warm warning tones, dense but legible operational
content, and no decorative rebrand. New views should feel like an editorial
field guide embedded in an industrial tool. Remain offline-first and use only
local assets and the existing typography and CSS system.

### Design Principles

1. Put the next safe human action before background explanation.
2. Keep EUVD intelligence, CRA decisions, SRP material readiness, official
   submission, and official receipts visually and semantically distinct.
3. Use progressive disclosure for legal context and exceptional cases while
   keeping deadlines, trigger criteria, and manual-only boundaries visible.
4. Treat dates and Q16 profiles as technical baselines, not permanent truth;
   always direct users to verify the current official portal before submission.
5. Support keyboard use, clear heading hierarchy, readable narrow layouts,
   printability, and reduced motion without hiding critical functions.
